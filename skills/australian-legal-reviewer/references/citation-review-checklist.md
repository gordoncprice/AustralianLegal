# Citation review checklist

For every material proposition:

1. Open the cited document rather than relying on a snippet or citation string.
2. Match title, jurisdiction, year, court/tribunal, neutral citation, date, provision or paragraph.
3. Confirm the pinpoint contains the claimed rule in context.
4. Confirm direct quotations word-for-word and keep them short.
5. Verify the document's official status and current or historical version.
6. Check whether an available primary source should replace secondary commentary.
7. Check later case treatment and legislation where material.
8. Ensure links resolve to the source, not only a search page, where practical.
9. Identify sources that were unavailable, paywalled, incomplete, or unofficial.
10. For a statutory proposition, verify the exact title and jurisdiction, section and subsection, operative wording, definitions, application and coverage, exceptions and exclusions, cross-references, commencement/current compilation, related provisions, and express or inferred character.
11. For a negative proposition, verify that the searched source set is authoritative and sufficiently complete; otherwise report `no matching official authority was located` and the search limit.

Use a proposition ledger:

| Proposition | Citation | Pinpoint verified | Authority/status | Currency/treatment | Defect/action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

An authentic citation can still be defective if it does not support the proposition. A correct proposition without traceable support remains unsupported.

Before issuing findings, add every proposition introduced by the reviewer to the ledger and verify it. Avoid claims that an authority is first, last, highest, only, or non-existent unless that exact proposition is verified. Prefer the minimum verified proposition needed to demonstrate the defect.
