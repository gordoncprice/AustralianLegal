# Source hierarchy and authority weight

Use this as an internal, non-numerical reasoning aid. Do not expose an authority score or band unless the user requests it. Weight is proposition-specific: validity, statutory text, interpretation, procedure, and regulator practice may require different sources.

## Controlling legal sources

- Australian Constitution for constitutional text.
- Current primary Commonwealth, state, or territory legislation for an operative statutory rule within its jurisdiction.
- Valid regulations and legislative instruments for rules made within delegated authority; check the empowering Act, status, commencement, application, disallowance and sunsetting.
- High Court holdings for controlling judicial interpretation.

Neither legislation nor case law mechanically outranks the other for every proposition: read operative text together with binding judicial interpretation. A delegated instrument can be binding law even though it is subordinate to its empowering Act.

## Judicial hierarchy

Within the relevant jurisdiction and subject to later treatment, ordinarily prefer:

1. High Court holdings.
2. Full Federal Court, state and territory Courts of Appeal, and other Full Courts.
3. Federal Court, state and territory Supreme Courts, and applicable industrial appellate or Full Bench authority.
4. Other single-instance courts, Fair Work Commission, Queensland Industrial Relations Commission, and specialist tribunals.

Check whether the proposition is a holding or observation, whether the court had jurisdiction, factual fit, later appeal or treatment, conflicting authority, and legislative change. Tribunal hierarchy and precedential effect must be verified rather than assumed from the institution's name.

## Interpretive and contextual support

- Explanatory memoranda, second-reading speeches, parliamentary material and law-reform reports may assist interpretation but are not substitutes for operative text or controlling holdings.
- Regulator interpretations, regulatory guides, government guidance, practice notes and procedural guidance may explain administration or practice but are not binding law unless a verified legal basis gives the particular material legal effect.
- Academic and professional commentary can aid discovery and analysis but should not carry a material proposition where stronger authority is reasonably available.
- Media, snippets, aggregators and informal discussion are discovery only.

For every substantive proposition, identify the strongest reasonably available authority. If relying on a lower-weight source despite an apparently stronger relevant source, explain the reason—for example, the stronger source does not decide the precise point, predates an amendment, is factually distinguishable, or is inaccessible—and adjust confidence. An official source is not automatically binding, and an unofficial repository should not silently replace an available authorised source.
