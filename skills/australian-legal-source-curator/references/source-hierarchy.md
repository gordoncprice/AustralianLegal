# Source hierarchy

Rank authority for the proposition and jurisdiction, not merely the publisher's reputation.

1. **Legislation and legislative instruments:** authorised current or historical versions, commencement instruments, gazettes, and official registers.
2. **Binding judicial authority:** authorised reports and official reasons from the controlling court hierarchy.
3. **Other official court and tribunal materials:** persuasive decisions, orders, practice material, and published tribunal reasons.
4. **Regulator and government material:** guidance, enforcement policy, registers, rulings, and administrative decisions; identify any statutory force precisely.
5. **Explanatory material:** explanatory memoranda, second-reading speeches, committee reports, and law-reform material.
6. **Parliamentary material:** Bills, amendments, Hansard, and other official records.
7. **Recognised secondary authority:** textbooks, journals, encyclopaedias, and expert commentary.
8. **Professional commentary:** law-firm and bar-association publications.
9. **Discovery-only material:** media, blogs, aggregators, snippets, and informal discussion.

An official source is not automatically binding. Regulator guidance remains guidance unless legislation gives the particular instrument legal effect. An unofficial repository can accurately reproduce binding law but should not silently replace an available authorised source.

For cases, apply court hierarchy, jurisdiction, ratio, later treatment, factual fit, and date. For legislation, apply version, commencement, territorial and personal application, amendments, transitions, and delegated instruments.
