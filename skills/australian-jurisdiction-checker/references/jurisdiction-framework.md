# Jurisdiction framework

## Decision sequence

1. **Characterise and prioritise the issue:** identify the core legal questions first. Put taxation, privacy, licensing, payroll tax, and other peripheral regimes under additional issues only when the facts make them material.
2. **Fix the connecting facts:** event location, work location, residence, employer location and legal identity, company registration and business activity, public/private sector, dates, instrument coverage, and forum.
3. **Test Commonwealth reach:** constitutional corporation, Commonwealth or territory employer, trade and commerce, referral legislation, national system coverage, federal statutory cause, and extraterritorial provisions.
4. **Test each local regime:** state or territory employment, industrial, long service leave, safety, workers' compensation, anti-discrimination, licensing, public-sector, and local-government rules.
5. **Classify the scheme:** exclusively Commonwealth; exclusively local; concurrent; cooperative/referral; applied; uniform/model but separately enacted; or uncertain.
6. **Test displacement and interaction:** express exclusions, preserved laws, inconsistency, concurrent operation, double recovery, choice of forum, and remedies.
7. **Map confidence by dimension:** distinguish a jurisdiction requiring investigation from legal-system coverage and the eventual substantive conclusion.

## National schemes are not identical rules

The Fair Work national system, model work health and safety laws, corporations legislation, privacy rules, and discrimination statutes have different constitutional foundations and local variations. Do not transfer coverage conclusions between them. A model law has effect only through the enacting jurisdiction's current text.

## Dates matter

Map jurisdiction as at the legally relevant event date as well as the research date. Referrals, institutional names, coverage rules, and local enactments can change. Identify transitional or savings rules when a regime changed between those dates.

## Confidence dimensions

Assess separately: jurisdiction identification; legal-system coverage; substantive legal conclusion; source completeness; and factual assumptions. For each use:

- **High:** the material facts and current authoritative provisions needed for that dimension are verified.
- **Moderate:** the position is likely but a material exception, classification, source gap, or cross-border point remains.
- **Preliminary/low:** essential facts or authoritative material are unavailable, or the issue is unsettled.

High confidence that Commonwealth, New South Wales, and Victoria require investigation does not establish high confidence that a particular Commonwealth or state law ultimately applies.
