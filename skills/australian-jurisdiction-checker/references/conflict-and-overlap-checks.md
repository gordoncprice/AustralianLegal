# Conflict and overlap checks

## Coverage audit

- Identify the legal person on each side; do not treat a brand or corporate group as the employer.
- Verify national-system coverage and any public-sector, local-government, sole-trader, partnership, or unincorporated-association exception.
- Separate minimum employment standards, awards/agreements, contract, long service leave, safety, workers' compensation, discrimination, and tax.
- Verify whether a state provision is preserved, excluded, referred, applied, or capable of concurrent operation.
- Treat model or uniform laws as separately enacted and potentially divergent.
- Identify temporal changes, transitional rules, and the law at the event date.

## Inconsistency and remedies

Do not announce constitutional inconsistency from first principles. Locate the relevant application, exclusion, preservation, displacement, or concurrent-operation provisions and controlling authority. Check:

- whether compliance with both laws is possible;
- whether one law intends to cover the field or preserve the other;
- whether the causes, duties, respondents, forums, time limits, and remedies differ;
- whether election, anti-duplication, or double-recovery rules apply; and
- whether procedural or jurisdictional limits make a theoretically available claim impractical.

## Draft audit questions

1. Does every Act and instrument belong to the identified jurisdiction and relevant date?
2. Does the cited provision apply to these parties, conduct, and location?
3. Has guidance been confused with legal coverage?
4. Are local exceptions to a national scheme disclosed?
5. Are cross-border and public-sector facts treated explicitly?
6. Is each exclusion supported rather than assumed?
