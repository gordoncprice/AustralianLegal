# Citation standard

Use the Australian Guide to Legal Citation where practicable, while prioritising traceability over cosmetic perfection.

## Legislation

Give the short title and year, jurisdiction abbreviation, and pinpoint provision: `Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 389`. Identify the compilation or historical version in the source note when currency or timing is material.

## Cases and tribunals

Give case name, authorised report citation where verified, neutral citation, court or tribunal, year/date as appropriate, and paragraph pinpoint. Hyperlink the case name and neutral citation directly to the best accessible full-text judgment, for example `[Example v Example [2026] HCA 1](https://court.example/judgment), [42]`. Do not invent an authorised report citation.

Prefer, in order: the official court judgment or authorised court publication; an official government or judicial repository; then AustLII, Jade or another reputable legal database where the official source is unavailable or impractical. Do not link a case citation to search results, a blog, news report, case summary, AI-generated page or secondary commentary when the judgment is available.

Before citing a case, open the link and verify that it resolves to the intended judgment; the name, neutral citation, court and year are correct; the pinpoint supports the precise proposition in context; later treatment has been checked; and the case remains relevant to the current statutory and doctrinal framework.

## Mandatory proposition-level coverage

Cite every material statement of legislation or regulation; statutory requirement; legal test; proposition, holding or reasoning drawn from a judgment; hierarchy or binding-effect characterisation; commencement, amendment, repeal or current-status claim; sourced factual observation; document or regulator-position characterisation; exception, qualification or limitation; application of law to supplied facts; analogy or distinction; substantive conclusion; authority-based confidence assessment; and statement that an issue is unresolved, uncertain or contested.

Clearly label facts supplied by the user; they need no external authority. Label an inference or application conclusion and connect it to the cited legal principle. Do not present an inference as if the source directly stated it. Pure headings and genuinely procedural transitions need no citation.

Place each citation immediately after the sentence or clause it supports. Do not use one citation at the end of a paragraph to imply support for distinct propositions unless it genuinely supports all of them. Split propositions into separate sentences or cite each clause where different sources apply. When grouping cases, state what each contributes or make clear that they support the same proposition.

If authoritative support for a material proposition is not located, omit it, qualify it explicitly as analysis or uncertainty, or state that supporting authority was not located. A topically related citation is not support.

## Other materials

Identify the issuing body, title, document type/date, pinpoint if available, official URL, and access/verification date when content is mutable. Label explanatory memoranda, parliamentary debates, regulator guidance, and commentary by type.

## Minimum checks

- Open the source and confirm title, jurisdiction, provision or paragraph, and text.
- Attribute every direct quotation and reproduce it exactly and sparingly.
- Ensure the source supports the full proposition, not merely adjacent words.
- Verify proposition-level coverage and immediate citation placement, including applications and conclusions.
- Distinguish primary, official secondary, and unofficial material.
- Prefer links to official pages; add a recognised repository only when useful.
- State that a primary source was not located rather than substituting an unattributed summary.
- Do not claim that a case, citation, source, or provision is the first, last, highest, only, or non-existent unless that exact proposition is established by an authoritative and sufficiently complete source.
