# Research method

## Search order

1. Define the proposition and jurisdiction before searching.
2. Locate current legislation on the official register. Read the whole relevant Part or Division, including application, definitions, notes, exceptions, remedies, and delegated instruments.
3. Open compilation, amendment, commencement, endnote, history, and transitional material. Record the version and relevant date.
4. Find authoritative cases through official court sources where available. Use recognised repositories and commentary to discover citations, then verify material text and metadata.
5. Search for later cases citing the authority and for later legislation affecting the rule. Record the search method and limits.
6. Locate official regulator guidance and enforcement material. Label it as guidance unless a legal instrument gives it another status.
7. Seek contrary or narrower formulations using statutory terms, neutral citations, provision numbers, and appellate treatment.

## Evidence ledger

Maintain a working ledger with: proposition; legal-effect label; source; pinpoint; jurisdiction; authority weight; stronger-authority check; current-status check; contrary material; source dependency; and confidence dimensions. Use `express statutory rule`, `judicial holding`, `judicial interpretation`, `judicial observation`, `regulator interpretation`, `procedural guidance`, `explanatory material`, `reasoned inference`, or `unresolved legal question` where useful. A search-result snippet is not evidence. Open the source.

For each material statutory proposition, check the exact title and jurisdiction, section and subsection, operative wording, relevant definitions, application and coverage, exceptions and exclusions, cross-references, commencement and current compilation, related provisions, and whether the statement is express or inferred.

## Issue-completeness review

Ask: `What legal issues could reasonably arise from these facts?` Consider, only where genuinely relevant: jurisdiction; applicable legal system; standing; coverage; definitions; limitation periods; commencement; transitional provisions; delegated legislation; industrial instruments; procedural obligations; notice; consultation; statutory elements; burden of proof; defences; exceptions; discretions; remedies; penalties; review and appeal rights; interaction with other legislation; regulator powers; recent appellate authority; conflicting authority; and unresolved questions. Record why a potentially material issue was included or excluded when the choice is not obvious. Do not force irrelevant discussion.

## Change detection

For each material issue, ask whether the law or official administration has materially changed in recent years. Check proportionately for amendments, commencement and transitional rules, recent High Court and relevant Full Court authority, significant appellate developments, and regulator policy changes. Record the search date, method and result. Highlight a development when it changes, qualifies, displaces, or creates uncertainty about an older rule.

## Source dependency

Record whether each material conclusion is supported by multiple independent authorities or primarily depends on one authority. Independence requires genuinely distinct support, not several pages repeating the same source. Where a conclusion depends on one appellate decision, recently amended text, or an unsettled point, verify treatment and current text closely, disclose the dependency, and calibrate confidence.

## Current-law protocol

- State the research date and event date.
- Verify in-force status and the correct compilation.
- Check assent/making separately from commencement.
- Identify provisions passed but not commenced.
- Inspect amendment and transitional schedules where material.
- For repealed or superseded law, explain whether savings preserve it for earlier facts.
- Check later judicial and legislative treatment of material cases.

## Uncertainty register

For each unresolved issue record: issue; why uncertain; potential legal impact; priority; and information required. Prioritise the facts or sources most capable of changing jurisdiction, coverage, liability, deadline, procedure, or remedy. Keep assumptions separate from conclusions.

## Failure handling

If an official source is unavailable, record the failure and use a recognised supplementary source with reduced confidence. Never reconstruct statutory wording, quotations, citations, or case treatment from memory. The failure to locate a source does not establish that it does not exist: state the sources and limits checked and use qualified language. If research falls outside the package's core fields, retain the same method, identify domain-specific sources, and disclose any expertise or source gap.
