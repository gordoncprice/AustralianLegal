# Final answer template

Use only the sections needed. Retain jurisdiction, authority, uncertainty, date, and disclaimer only to the extent material to the question. Internal workflow artefacts and review findings are not separate final-answer sections unless requested.

1. **Research question** — neutral formulation.
2. **Brief answer** — direct and calibrated. Put an outcome-determinative assumption or unresolved coverage issue before the conclusion it qualifies.
3. **Jurisdiction and governing framework** — likely and overlapping regimes, cited basis, assumptions, and separate confidence in jurisdiction identification and legal-system coverage.
4. **Principal authorities** — a concise table or narrative giving each materially relied-upon case's hyperlinked name and neutral citation, court and precedential status, proposition supported, analytical relevance, and verified pinpoint where appropriate. Omit cumulative cases.
5. **Relevant legal framework** — current primary rules and their status, cited at proposition level.
6. **Legal analysis** — supported proposition and legal-effect label, application or labelled inference, limitation, source dependency and conclusion. Place citations immediately after the sentence or clause supported.
7. **Contrary or limiting authority** — significant competing, distinguishing or limiting authority where it could materially affect the result.
8. **Important exceptions or qualifications** — exclusions, fact sensitivity and transitions.
9. **Recent developments** — material amendments, commencement/transitional issues, appellate developments, or regulator policy changes.
10. **Practical next steps** — immediate factual enquiries, documents required, information still needed, critical deadlines and potential procedural risks, without definitive tailored advice.
11. **Uncertainties and additional facts** — only outcome-determinative and materially relevant gaps: issue, reason, potential legal impact, priority, information required, and authority where the uncertainty depends on law or sourced material.
12. **Conclusion** — supported by the controlling principles and strongest application authorities rather than an uncited summary.
13. **Additional issues if relevant** — peripheral regimes only when facts make them material.
14. **Sources** — traceable primary sources first, then labelled official guidance and secondary material; disclose material single-authority dependency.
15. **Research date** — date currency and links were checked.
16. **Legal information disclaimer** — concise and proportionate.

For a narrow question, combine items 1–8 into a brief answer plus material qualifications and sources. For a simple statutory question, answer in a few paragraphs: direct rule, controlling provision, material assumption or exception, research date and concise disclaimer. For a next-24-hours request, use `Essential now` and `Useful follow-up`; prioritise documents, dates, preservation and immediate instructions and avoid a general legal memorandum. For a formal memorandum, retain full headings and an executive summary.

The heading structure is optional; the underlying coverage is not. Cite each material rule, sourced observation, authority characterisation, application, qualification, uncertainty and conclusion at the point of support. Clearly distinguish supplied facts and labelled inferences.
