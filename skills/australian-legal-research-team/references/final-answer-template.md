# Final answer template

Use only the sections needed. Retain jurisdiction, authority, uncertainty, date, and disclaimer only to the extent material to the question. Internal workflow artefacts and review findings are not separate final-answer sections unless requested.

1. **Research question** — neutral formulation.
2. **Brief answer** — direct and calibrated. Put an outcome-determinative assumption or unresolved coverage issue before the conclusion it qualifies.
3. **Applicable jurisdiction** — likely and overlapping regimes, assumptions, and separate confidence in jurisdiction identification and legal-system coverage.
4. **Relevant legal framework** — current primary rules and their status.
5. **Analysis** — proposition and legal-effect label, strongest reasonably available authority, application, limitation, source dependency, conclusion.
6. **Important exceptions or qualifications** — exclusions, contrary authority, fact sensitivity, transitions.
7. **Recent developments** — material amendments, commencement/transitional issues, appellate developments, or regulator policy changes.
8. **Practical next steps** — immediate factual enquiries, documents required, information still needed, critical deadlines and potential procedural risks, without definitive tailored advice.
9. **Uncertainty register** — include only outcome-determinative and materially relevant gaps: issue, reason, potential legal impact, priority and information required. Omit secondary evidentiary gaps unless requested.
10. **Additional issues if relevant** — peripheral regimes only when facts make them material.
11. **Sources** — traceable primary sources first, then labelled official guidance and secondary material; disclose material single-authority dependency.
12. **Research date** — date currency and links were checked.
13. **Legal information disclaimer** — concise and proportionate.

For a narrow question, combine items 1–8 into a brief answer plus material qualifications and sources. For a simple statutory question, answer in a few paragraphs: direct rule, controlling provision, material assumption or exception, research date and concise disclaimer. For a next-24-hours request, use `Essential now` and `Useful follow-up`; prioritise documents, dates, preservation and immediate instructions and avoid a general legal memorandum. For a formal memorandum, retain full headings and an executive summary.
